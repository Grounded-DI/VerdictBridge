# VerdictBridge

## What Can This Record Actually Support?

**VerdictBridge is a litigation-record sufficiency and state-control system developed by Grounded DI LLC.**

It does not try to predict a jury through a single probability score.

VerdictBridge evaluates the legal and evidentiary conditions inside a particular case and asks:

- What is established?
- What remains disputed?
- What depends on expert testimony?
- What evidence is missing, vulnerable, or unresolved?
- Which claims, defenses, and damages theories have sufficient record support?
- Which procedural paths remain available?
- What blocks motion readiness, mediation readiness, trial readiness, or appellate readiness?
- What changes when one material input changes?
- What remains stable after that change?
- Is the resulting analysis ready for attorney reliance?

> **Historical analytics estimates from other cases. VerdictBridge evaluates what the present record can actually support.**

## The Core Question

Most litigation systems begin with one of these questions:

- What is the governing law?
- What happened in similar cases?
- How has this judge ruled?
- What verdicts or settlements appear in the historical data?
- How can the documents be reviewed?
- How can the brief, chronology, or case assessment be drafted?

VerdictBridge begins with a different question:

> **Is the litigation record and resulting analysis sufficiently supported, internally coherent, evidentially grounded, and reviewed to be treated as ready for attorney reliance?**

A polished memo is not necessarily motion-ready.

A correct calculation may remain unusable because its inputs are contested.

A valid legal rule may remain unusable because the required factual predicate is missing.

A completed analysis may remain unreleased because attorney review, carrier review, replay, or authorization has not occurred.

---

## From Documents to Litigation States

VerdictBridge treats readiness as a condition of the record—not as a label inferred from the amount of work completed.

An issue or conclusion may be:

- **Supported** — the present record supplies the required type of proof.
- **Disputed** — competing positions remain visible.
- **Expert-dependent** — the conclusion requires qualified and methodologically supported opinion.
- **Evidentially incomplete** — a required document, foundation, authentication, or item of testimony is absent.
- **Held** — the issue remains available for later analysis but cannot presently be treated as established.
- **Motion-ready** — the law, record, posture, and review support dispositive-motion analysis.
- **Mediation-ready** — the principal support, disputes, dependencies, and bounded exposure conditions are sufficiently understood.
- **Trial-ready** — evidence, instructions, rulings, and trial issues are sufficiently developed for the identified purpose.
- **Appellate-review ready** — preservation events, objections, rulings, exhibits, transcripts, and instructions are sufficiently complete.
- **Not ready for reliance** — the analysis may be useful, but unresolved verification or approval prevents the proposed use.

A case does not have to occupy one universal state.

Liability may be sufficiently developed for mediation while:

- a future-medical claim remains held;
- a comparative-fault theory remains expert-dependent;
- a damages model remains provisional; and
- the full matter remains unready for final attorney reliance.

## Visible Proof in the Turner Demonstration

### Motorcycle speed

The demonstration preserved:

- competing plaintiff and defense estimates;
- missing methodology;
- unresolved causal effect; and
- the absence of an adjudicated fault percentage.

When a stronger defense-speed predicate was tested, comparative-fault-adjusted calculations changed.

The injuries, gross medical damages, wage record, and coverage structure did not.

### Future knee replacement

The system recognized that future knee replacement could materially affect high-side exposure.

It nevertheless kept the procedure held because:

- medical probability was incomplete;
- timing was unresolved;
- current imaging was not supplied; and
- the cost model depended on the disputed medical predicate.

When the support status changed in a replay branch, future-care arithmetic changed.

Liability and wage conclusions did not.

### Future earning capacity

The demonstration preserved the tension between:

- alleged physical restrictions; and
- a return to full-time work at the same hourly rate.

It did not force a choice between zero future loss and the highest plaintiff model.

It maintained separate theories with separate evidentiary requirements and support states.

## Controlled Replay

Litigation analysis changes because the record changes.

Examples include:

- an expert is excluded;
- a document is authenticated;
- a treating physician supplies a missing probability opinion;
- payroll records alter a wage calculation;
- a ruling narrows the case;
- an admission changes liability;
- a damages input changes; or
- a jury instruction changes the path to verdict.

VerdictBridge evaluates:

1. which conclusion depended on the changed input;
2. what must be recalculated;
3. what remains unchanged;
4. what litigation paths open or close;
5. what remains held or unresolved; and
6. whether the updated analysis is ready for review.

> **VerdictBridge does not let one new fact silently rewrite the entire case. It shows which conclusions depend on the change and which do not.**

A trajectory is not a guess about the future.

It is a conditional map of the litigation states that remain available if identified conditions are satisfied.

## Public Audit Chain

The Turner demonstration presents the public workflow as:

> **Authorized task → structured input → classified proposition → contradiction review → evidentiary or legal review → litigation analysis → candidate output → replay or validation → reviewer decision → release state**

The book distinguishes three events that are often blurred together:

> **Generating an output, validating an output, and authorizing reliance on an output are different events.**

The completed demonstration remained:

- **VerdictReady:** No
- **FilingReady:** Not applicable or not yet determinable for a later filing
- **Export:** Held

That did not mean the work lacked value.

It meant that useful analysis was not being mislabeled as verified, approved, or authorized.

## What VerdictBridge Is Not

VerdictBridge is not presented as:

- a statistical jury oracle;
- a guaranteed verdict predictor;
- a substitute for attorney judgment;
- a settlement-authority generator;
- a reserve-setting directive;
- a coverage determination;
- an automated legal decision-maker;
- a guarantee of motion, mediation, trial, or appellate success; or
- a claim that uncertainty can be eliminated from litigation.

VerdictBridge is designed to preserve uncertainty where uncertainty exists and show what additional proof, review, or authorization is required.

## Relationship to BriefWise

VerdictBridge and BriefWise address related but different control questions.

### VerdictBridge

> **Is the litigation record and analysis sufficiently supported for the proposed litigation decision or next state?**

VerdictBridge focuses on:

- liability;
- evidence;
- testimony;
- contradictions;
- experts;
- damages;
- comparative fault;
- procedural posture;
- exposure;
- replay;
- and litigation readiness.

### BriefWise

> **Is the resulting legal proposition sufficiently supported and verified for a filing or other court-facing use?**

BriefWise focuses on:

- authority quality;
- proposition support;
- citation accuracy;
- quotation fidelity;
- jurisdiction;
- procedural posture;
- adverse treatment;
- jury instructions; and
- filing-integrity review.
